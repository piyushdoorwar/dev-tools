What AA and AAA mean

The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) define three conformance levels. They are not scores out of three — each one is a different bar, and a colour pair either clears it or does not.

  • A — the minimum level. It places no contrast requirement on text at all, so it is not shown here.
  • AA — the level law and policy actually reference: EN 301 549, which the European Accessibility Act has enforced since June 2025, and Section 508 in the US. Treat this as the bar to clear.
  • AAA — the enhanced level, roughly "comfortable for low vision without assistive tech". WCAG itself advises against requiring it across a whole site, because some content cannot reach it. Aim for it on body text where the palette allows.

Thresholds

  • 4.5:1 — AA for normal text (WCAG 2.2, 1.4.3).
  • 3:1 — AA for large text (24px, or 18.66px bold) and for UI components and graphics (1.4.11).
  • 7:1 — AAA for normal text, 4.5:1 for large text (1.4.6).

Notes

  • A translucent foreground is composited over the background before the ratio is measured, because that is what the eye actually sees.
  • OKLCH lightness is perceptual, so Nudge to AA moves only L and leaves hue and chroma alone.
  • An oklch() value outside the sRGB gamut is clipped for the HEX, RGB and HSL rows; the OKLCH row keeps what you typed.
